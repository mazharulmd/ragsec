\textbf{Why the manuscript is in scope for IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and
Security.}

\emph{Expert Systems with Applications} publishes work on the design and application of expert
and intelligent systems. This manuscript does not propose or apply such a system. Its subject
is information security: the trust assumption behind cryptographic authentication of documents
entering a retrieval pipeline. It contributes (i) an adversary model in which an authorized
signer is compromised, (ii) the measurement that authenticated ingestion gives no protection
against that adversary, (iii) an aggregation rule that counts corroboration over the signer
identity verified by the ingestion service, with a stated bound on the weight one principal
can contribute, and (iv) an attack on the binding of that identity showing that an
uploader-chosen attribute, even one covered by the signature, cannot define a principal. These
are questions of authentication, provenance, and resistance to Sybil-style fabrication of
support, which fall within information security as covered by the Transactions. The language
model is the system being protected and is not the object of the contribution.

The manuscript was reorganized for this submission so that these security contributions are
its core, and the earlier layered-defense ablation is reduced to the baseline against which
they are measured.
