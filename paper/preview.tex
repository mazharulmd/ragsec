\title{RAG Security and Poisoning Attack Defense in LLM-Based Systems:\\
A Layered Architecture and Its Limits Under an Adaptive Adversary}
\author{Mazharul Islam}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) moves part of a language model's trust boundary out of its
weights and into a document pipeline, exposing deployments to knowledge-base poisoning,
indirect prompt injection, trigger backdoors and retrieval flooding. We present an instrumented
RAG framework with a four-layer defense---ingestion-time source verification, calibrated
retrieval filtering, provenance-weighted robust aggregation and anomaly monitoring---in which
each layer can be enabled independently, and evaluate it on SQuAD, HotpotQA and a bundled
corpus with a sentence-transformer retriever across seven configurations, five attacks and
three seeds. Against four static attacks reproduced from the literature, the layered defense
performs as that literature would predict. We then introduce a defense-aware adaptive adversary
composed of six independently switchable evasion techniques. Against it, the same stack fails:
the retrieval filter's true-positive rate falls to zero and its AUROC to chance, and robust
aggregation actively increases attack success because the adversary supplies the corroboration
its redundancy weighting rewards. A leave-one-out ablation attributes the failure to exactly
three of the six techniques, each a cheap rewriting choice, and a budget sweep prices the attack
at two documents per target. No detector threshold serves both adversaries. Binding ingestion to
an unforgeable HMAC signature restores the defense at no cost to clean accuracy, and is the only
control in our study that survives.
\end{abstract}
